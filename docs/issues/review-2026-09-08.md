# CogniGraph product status and Rust review — 2026-09-08

## Assessment

CogniGraph 2.6.0 is an implemented Native-first graph/document retrieval product with CGQL, persistent storage, text/vector/graph search, Lua scripting, semantic construction, and a substantial signed evaluation and deployment control plane. It is beyond a parser or research prototype. Its strongest engineering investment is the explicit authority chain from evaluation inputs to signed selection and, in M26, a separately signed atomic Native deployment.

The review nevertheless found correctness and isolation defects at the boundaries between those systems. The most urgent are the Lua instruction-limit bypass, unsafe derivative paths, directed ingestion bypassing the deployed-generation fence, malformed directed output replacing valid facts, and tenant/paged-cache lifecycle failures. Passing the existing suite does not establish these invariants: several failures were reproduced against the real server. The [issue registry](README.md) contains the individual findings and closure criteria.

This report records the initial audit snapshot: all 30 findings were Open, and no production deployment, commit, push, or Rust implementation change was made during that audit. Subsequent remediation is tracked in the [current registry](README.md) and [first fix batch](fixes-2026-09-08.md).

## Delivered direction and explicit limits

The plan marks M1–M26 delivered. That means the phases and their dated acceptance evidence exist; it does not mean this audit found no defects.

| Area | Delivered state | Boundary that remains intentional |
|---|---|---|
| Native/query foundation | Persistence, atomic batches, search, public CGQL, multiple FOR/LET/subqueries, functions and workload-driven optimizations | No new third-party backend strategy; no cost-based join reordering, general hash joins, UDFs, or HA replication. Supported expression-subquery amendments supersede the old broad LET-only exclusion. |
| Semantic Neurons | Proposed/accepted/rejected/retired symbolic aliases, hints, blockers, and rank hints; effective configuration affects grounding/ranking | Proposed records are inert. These are inspectable symbolic rules, not neural units, learned weights, or a guarantee that every inferred fact is true. |
| M15–M17 | Foundation guards, revision-safe occurrence reconciliation, result invalidation, durable tenant-scoped jobs, replay keys, quotas/cursors, fairness, recovery and archival | Singleton process and store. Cancellation follows checkpoints; an evaluation or large batch can delay it. Unknown quota keys are stored, not automatically enforced. |
| M18–M20 | Four-job candidate/baseline and replay evaluation evidence, promotion gates, independently signed policy/approval/promotion duties, external artifact attestations | Promotion selects an evaluated candidate. Signatures attest a scoped act/claim; they do not prove scientific validity or availability of external bytes. |
| M21–M23 | Actual verified CAS consumption, deterministic graph derivation from typed candidate/prepared corpus, exact UTF-8 raw-document preparation | No arbitrary artifact execution, model-based qualification, document-format parser/OCR pipeline, or automatic graph switch. Inputs and algorithms are closed and bounded. |
| M24 | Evidence-derived custody plans, verified offline backup bundles, complete absent-scope restoration, receipts | Online CAS remains read-only. Restoration has explicit platform/publication constraints and is not a database restore by itself. |
| M25 | Signed immutable semantic revision, independent review, selection through the existing promotion head, explicit governed ingest | One governed ingest call is an atomic chunk batch; several calls do not constitute a corpus-wide deployment. |
| M26 | Immutable complete occurrence generations, candidate/baseline impact, separately signed Native atomic deployment and explicit rollback | No deployment scheduler, automatic drift healing, multi-writer leases, replication, or generation garbage collection. Arango fails before unsupported materialization writes. Directed-route closure is currently defective (CG-3). |
| Side-views | Asynchronous generated question/answer retrieval expansion with separately configurable completion provider | Non-authoritative retrieval data; it must not become facts. Orphan prevention across deletion paths is incomplete (CG-13). Parent updates intentionally require manual regeneration. |

## Semantic-neuron evidence and fixtures

The research trail is useful because it records both improvements and failed generalization. Authored-kit construction/repair scores demonstrate the mechanics on those kits. They do not establish domain-independent answer quality. The recorded real-label comparison (vector 36%, graph 22%) contradicts generalizing the authored answer advantage (graph 86%, vector 36%). Coverage, subject binding, negation, direction, and chunking are measurable failure sources rather than problems eliminated by the existence of a graph.

The later D12 route accepts a caller-owned taxonomy and nominates per-document facts through a completion provider, then applies deterministic evidence gates and the shared occurrence writer. Its CUAD holdout score is a reported result (P 0.744, R 0.635, F1 0.685 on 40 contracts), not a freshly reproduced result in this audit. The missing reproduction package is CG-25. The same decision records that semantic quarantine did not selectively improve precision for that directed legal task; that limit should remain visible.

| Fixture/evidence family | What it establishes or exercises |
|---|---|
| `fixtures/semantic-neurons/{space_types,chunks,evaluations,neurons}` | Authored spaces, evidence chunks, expected/forbidden fact definitions, accepted/proposed configuration. |
| `fixtures/semantic-neurons/blind` and `hostile` | Independent authored/gap-bearing kits and adversarial restraint; the corrected distinct-fact denominators are the reference for CG-24. |
| `fixtures/semantic-neurons/judge-injection` and `judge-replay` | Recorded reviewer behavior, prompt-injection screening, disagreement and agreement-policy results; not a blanket prompt-injection guarantee. |
| `fixtures/semantic-neurons/dailymed`, generalization and real-label artifacts | Domain transfer, drift, clinical calibration/coverage and answer comparison. Clinical expert-reference completion remains a separate evidence requirement. |
| `crates/cognigraph-construct/fixtures/webnlg` | Mined/proposed/pruned artifacts, with scoring decisions and experiment limits in the WebNLG documentation. Several current paths/status summaries need reconciliation. |
| `fixtures/m16`–`fixtures/m20`, `m22`–`m26`, and `docs/examples/m21-consumption-plan.json` | Wire examples, signing bindings, preparation goldens, custody and deployment requests. Fixtures with placeholder signatures are examples, not independently valid production authority. |
| Query corpus and Native/backend tests | Deterministic parser/validation/execution examples and cross-backend contracts. Passing them does not cover the alternate-path and concurrency defects found here. |

Historical Python-era Semantic Neurons documents were not treated as broken Rust integration instructions when they explicitly describe the research implementation. Publication drafts and historical experiment outputs were not rewritten during the audit.

The test count is Cargo's reported count, not a count of live external integrations. Some Arango/provider/LLM tests return early unless environment gates are supplied and are therefore not reported as `ignored`. A live Arango instance, external paid models, optional ONNX/provider feature combinations, Docker/release packaging, and production deployment were not independently verified in this audit. Historical decision entries contain earlier release/live evidence, which is not substituted for a new run.

HTTP probes used loopback only, synthetic credentials, clean temporary working directories, embedding provider `none` where appropriate, and a local completion stub. The bounded Lua probe used a finite loop. Test servers were stopped. The retained [HTTP result JSON](../evidence/engineering-historical-checks.md#artifact-b88e1642e95b63187a57) contains no credentials. It also includes an exploratory empty/malformed diagnostic evaluation: that legacy diagnostic behavior is explicitly distinguished from promotion scoring in the M18 decision and was not registered as a new defect.

Additional lifecycle observations:

- After tenant deletion/recreation, searching `newvocabulary` returned zero rows while `oldvocabulary` returned the new `notes/same` document. The old JWT was rejected with 401, and only the old redb file was quarantined.
- After importing a synthetic side-view and deleting its parent through `/api/batch`, the side-view remained readable.
- In paged/sidecar mode, dropping `notes` returned success, but a later GET of its previously cached `a` document still returned HTTP 200.

Source-confirmed findings identify their unexecuted concurrency schedules or external-backend checks in their own tickets. They are not presented as live reproductions. No claim is made that an audit can prove the absence of all other defects.

## Remediation order

1. Close the execution and isolation boundaries: CG-1/2/3/4/6/11/12/14, then verify the same paths through real HTTP and restart/recreation.
2. Restore evidence/query/storage consistency: canonical text, resolver parity, UPSERT predicates, paged concurrency, side-view orphan prevention, and backend contracts.
3. Reconcile the public contract and research evidence, then split the largest modules in bounded behavior-preserving changes. Keep each issue open until its own acceptance criteria are met.
