Clinical reference — reviewer

Labeling rules — R1–R7 (frozen at calibration; read before starting)
  • R1 — Concept preservation (governing). Drop a qualifier only when it does not change the concept's extension. Subtype, severity, anatomy, timing, causative drug, and treatment combination are essential. A lossy broad candidate is false; add the exact qualified concept as true. ("Known" may be dropped; the allergen may not.) systemic fungal infection ≠ infection; partial-onset seizures ≠ seizures; hypersensitivity to pregabalin ≠ hypersensitivity.
  • R2 — Adjunctive therapy counts as treatment, but the target keeps its subtype.
  • R3 — Treated target, not associated context. "adjunctive therapy in edema associated with congestive heart failure" is TREATS edema…, not TREATS heart failure.
  • R4 — Contraindications decided on clinical meaning, never phrasing. Bare list items and "should not be used in…" are valid contraindications.
  • R5 — Combination contraindications bind the combination. "Do not co-administer aliskiren with olmesartan in patients with diabetes" does not make diabetes a contraindication for olmesartan alone.
  • R6 — Restricted/refractory-only indications do not support a plain TREATS; mark the plain concept false and add the qualified one.
  • R7 — Boilerplate is not an indication (e.g. antibiotic-stewardship language).

For every candidate: mark true / false / uncertain. Then read the full section and add any real fact the candidates missed — the reference must be exhaustive for these two relations. Evidence for an added fact must be copied exactly from the section text. Do not consult any tool output. Work independently of the other reviewer.
